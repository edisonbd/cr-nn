# CR-NN Mathematics (fixed definitions)

This document pins down all mathematical definitions used by the CR-NN project,
as the single reference for implementation. Any ambiguity in the implementation
is resolved by this document; corrections here must be explicitly versioned.
Stability grades for every assertion are in `assumptions.md`.

> **Notation**: unless stated otherwise, $n$ is the CR complex dimension,
> $Q=2n+2$ the homogeneous dimension, and $\mathbb{H}^n$ the
> $(2n+1)$-dimensional Heisenberg group.

---

## 1. The CR manifold: the Heisenberg group $\mathbb{H}^n$

### 1.1 Group structure and coordinates

The Heisenberg group $\mathbb{H}^n = \mathbb{C}^n \times \mathbb{R}$ has elements
$g=(z,t)$, $z=x+iy\in\mathbb{C}^n$, $t\in\mathbb{R}$, with multiplication

$$
(z,t)\cdot(z',t') = \left(z+z',\ t+t' + 2\,\mathrm{Im}(z\cdot \bar{z}')\right)
$$

where $z\cdot\bar z'=\sum_{j=1}^n z_j\overline{z'_j}$. In real coordinates
$(x,y,t)$ this is

$$
(x,y,t)\cdot(x',y',t')=(x+x',\ y+y',\ t+t' + 2(x\cdot y' - y\cdot x')).
$$

**Why this group**: $\mathbb{H}^n$ is the simplest non-abelian nilpotent Lie
group; its boundary CR structure is exactly the boundary of the Siegel upper
half-space in $\mathbb{C}^{n+1}$. It is the prototype of a "flat" CR manifold —
the Webster curvature vanishes identically and the sub-Laplacian has a closed-form
spectrum.

### 1.2 Horizontal distribution and complex structure

The horizontal sub-bundle of the tangent space is spanned by the left-invariant
vector fields

$$
X_j = \partial_{x_j} + 2y_j\,\partial_t,\qquad
Y_j = \partial_{y_j} - 2x_j\,\partial_t,\qquad j=1,\dots,n.
$$

The centre direction $T=\partial_t$ is not in the horizontal distribution (this
is the source of sub-ellipticity). The non-vanishing bracket

$$
[X_j,Y_j] = -4\,\partial_t = -4T
$$

satisfies the Hörmander condition, so the sub-Laplacian generated by
$X_j,Y_j$ is hypoelliptic rather than elliptic.

**The complex structure** $J$ on the horizontal distribution is

$$
J X_j = Y_j,\qquad J Y_j = -X_j.
$$

Write $Z_j = \tfrac{1}{2}(X_j - iY_j)$ (type $(1,0)$) and
$\bar Z_j=\tfrac{1}{2}(X_j+iY_j)$ (type $(0,1)$).

### 1.3 Homogeneous dimension

Under the dilation $\delta_r(z,t)=(rz, r^2 t)$, the homogeneous dimension of
$\mathbb{H}^n$ is

$$
Q = 2n + 2,
$$

not the topological dimension $2n+1$. $Q$ appears in the homogeneity of all
kernels (e.g. the Korányi kernel is homogeneous of degree $-Q$).

## 2. CR operators

### 2.1 The tangential Cauchy–Riemann operator $\bar\partial_b$

$$
\bar\partial_b f = \sum_{j=1}^n \bar Z_j f\ d\bar z_j
   = \tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^n (X_j + iY_j)f\ d\bar z_j.
$$

**CR functions**: $\bar\partial_b f = 0$. This is the intrinsic notion of
"holomorphic" on $\mathbb{H}^n$. The formal adjoint is $\bar\partial_b^*$; their
composition gives the Kohn sub-Laplacian (see 2.3).

> **Project semantics**: pushing the representation toward CR functions is
> equivalent to compressing information into the holomorphic (complex) subspace
> — the geometric carrier of "information aggregated to the complex dimension"
> (assumption A3).

### 2.2 Discrete realization of the horizontal vector fields

The continuous fields $X_j,Y_j$ are realized in one of two equivalent ways (the
choice is recorded in the operator implementation comments):

- **Finite differences**: on the $(x,y,t)$ lattice, central differences of the
  form $X_j f \approx [f(\cdot+2y_j\Delta t)-f(\cdot-2y_j\Delta t)]/(2\Delta x)$.
  Simple, but destroys the exact spectral structure.
- **Spectral realization (preferred)**: after Fourier transform along the centre
  direction, $X_j,Y_j$ act on each $\lambda$-slice as multiplication by
  $i\xi_j$, $\partial_{z_j}$, etc., exactly on the Hermite basis. **The
  implementation defaults to the spectral realization**, so that unit tests of
  $\Delta_b$ on eigenfunctions reproduce the spectrum.

### 2.3 The sub-Laplacian $\Delta_b$ (Kohn sub-Laplacian)

$$
\Delta_b = -\sum_{j=1}^n (X_j^2 + Y_j^2) = -2\sum_{j=1}^n (Z_j\bar Z_j + \bar Z_j Z_j).
$$

(Equivalently $\Delta_b = 2\,\bar\partial_b^*\bar\partial_b$ in a suitable
normalization; the constant is fixed and tested in `operators.py`.)

**Hypoelliptic, not elliptic**: $\Delta_b$ contains no $\partial_t^2$, but the
Hörmander condition still makes it hypoelliptic, with a $1/2$-order (rather than
$1$-order) regularity gain.

## 3. Spectral structure (flat model — the most solid part)

### 3.1 Central Fourier decomposition

Fourier-transforming along $t$, $f\mapsto\hat f(\cdot,\lambda)$ with
$\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}$, the sub-Laplacian reduces to a family of
scaled harmonic oscillators:

$$
\widehat{\Delta_b f}(\,\cdot\,,\lambda) = \mathcal L_\lambda\,\hat f(\,\cdot\,,\lambda),\qquad
\mathcal L_\lambda = -\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}} + \lambda^2|x|^2 + i\lambda\,(x\cdot\nabla_y - y\cdot\nabla_x).
$$

i.e. a Hermite operator at scale $|\lambda|$ plus a complex phase term.

### 3.2 Eigenvalues

The eigenvalues of $\mathcal L_\lambda$ (**the project's core spectral formula**):

$$
\boxed{\;\sigma_{k,\lambda} = (2k+n)\,|\lambda|,\qquad k=0,1,2,\dots\;}
$$

with eigenfunctions the rescaled Hermite functions (scaled by $\lambda$, with the
special Hermite–Laguerre angular structure); multiplicity $n$ over $j=1,\dots,n$.

> This is the entire mathematical basis for "the flat model is fast": all
> spectral operations reduce to this diagonalization.

### 3.3 Truncation

On the non-compact $\mathbb{H}^n$ the spectrum is continuous in $\lambda$ and
discrete in $k$. In the implementation:

- $\lambda$: discretized to $L$ frequency points (given automatically by the
  centre-direction FFT).
- $k$: truncated to the first $K$ modes ($k=0,\dots,K-1$), a low-frequency
  approximation.
- Truncation error $O(e^{-cK})$ (Hermite functions decay rapidly), recorded in
  `spectrum.py`.

> Compact alternative: if the non-compact truncation causes problems, one can
> switch to the discrete spectrum on the sphere $S^{2n+1}$. Recorded as a
> fallback.

## 4. Kernels (closed forms)

### 4.1 The Korányi kernel

$$
\Gamma(g) = c_n\,\rho(g)^{-Q},\qquad
\rho(z,t) = \bigl(|z|^4 + t^2\bigr)^{1/4},
$$

with $c_n$ as in Folland (1975) so that $\Delta_b\Gamma=\delta_0$. $\rho$ is the
norm of the Korányi–Cygan distance. This is the fundamental solution of
$\Delta_b$, homogeneous of degree $-Q=-(2n+2)$.

More generally the Riesz kernels $I_\alpha(g)=C_{n,\alpha}\,\rho(g)^{\alpha-Q}$.

### 4.2 The Szegő projection kernel (flat closed form)

On the boundary of the Siegel upper half-space (the CR structure of
$\mathbb{H}^n$), the Szegő projection $\Pi$ has kernel

$$
S(g) = c_n'\,\bigl(|z|^2 - i t\bigr)^{-(n+1)}.
$$

(Here $|z|^2-it$ is complex-valued, the power taken on the principal branch;
the relation to the Korányi kernel is that $|\,|z|^2-it\,|^2 = |z|^4+t^2$.)

**Use**: the Szegő projection is the orthogonal projection onto CR-holomorphic
functions — i.e. "remove the non-holomorphic part". This is the intrinsic
aggregation operator of the CR-Attention layer.

### 4.3 Numerical notes (must-read for implementation)

- **Singularity**: as $g\to 0$, $\Gamma\sim\rho^{-Q}$ and $S\sim\rho^{-2(n+1)}$
  diverge. Regularize with $\rho^2\to\rho^2+\eta$, $\eta$ a small positive
  number (default $10^{-6}$).
- **Complex-power branch cut**: $(|z|^2-it)^{-(n+1)}$ on the principal branch.
  The branch conventions of the two backends' `log`/`pow` must be aligned
  explicitly, or gradients explode.
- **Convolution speed**: compute $S*f$ by FFT convolution (the group structure
  makes it a group convolution), reducing to $O(N\log N)$. **Never** the naive
  $O(N^2)$.

## 5. Perturbation expansion: the hybrid scheme

### 5.1 Positioning (important)

The project adopts the hybrid "flat fast transform + learnable curvature
perturbation". Its correct mathematical positioning is:

> A **truncated perturbation expansion**, *not* a "low-rank correction". The
> curvature perturbation is generally a full-rank local symbol perturbation;
> there is no literature supporting "curvature = low rank". Away from the flat
> model, no exact $O(N\log N)$ spectral transform exists; the "speed" here comes
> from a truncated approximation plus learned compensation, with controllable but
> nonzero error.

Theory: small-curvature perturbation expansions of the sub-Riemannian heat/Szegő
kernels (Barilari arXiv:1105.1285; Boutet de Monvel–Sjöstrand FIO asymptotics).

### 5.2 Form

Let $S_\text{flat}$ be the flat Szegő projection of §4.2. The Szegő projection on
a curved pseudo-Hermitian manifold is approximated by

$$
S_\text{curved} \;\approx\; S_\text{flat} \;+\; \sum_{j=1}^{M} \varepsilon^j\, L_j[S_\text{flat}],
$$

where
- $M$ is the truncation order (hyperparameter, default $M=2$);
- $\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}$ is a learnable small parameter (curvature amplitude,
  initialized $0$, soft-constrained $|\varepsilon|<\varepsilon_\text{max}=0.1$);
- $L_j$ is the $j$-th-order transport operator, whose symbol is determined by
  Webster curvature invariants. In the implementation $L_j$ takes a parametrized
  form (learnable coefficients on low-order symbols of $\Delta_b$), not an exact
  geometric transport — this is the engineering of "the learned term absorbs the
  truncation remainder".

### 5.3 Error and risk

- The truncation error is formally $O(\varepsilon^{M+1})$, absorbed by the
  learnable parameters of $L_j$.
- **Known open risks** (recorded in `assumptions.md`):
  - The sub-Riemannian **logarithmic correction** $\log\rho$ may land on the
    truncation order and cannot simply be treated as a power; it is flagged
    separately and can be disabled.
  - **Eigenvalue multiplicity splitting**: under small curvature the spectrum is
    continuous but degenerate; if the fast transform relies on the Hermite
    multiplicity structure, training must tolerate splitting.
  - **Global vs local frequency bands**: the perturbation expansion is local
    (small-time / high-frequency); low-frequency large-scale behaviour deviates
    more, and the network's operating band must be assessed.

## 6. CR-Sobolev loss

### 6.1 Definition

The Sobolev norm defined by the sub-Laplacian spectrum (the most solid loss
component):

$$
\|f\|^2_{S_b^s} = \sum_{k,\lambda} (1 + \sigma_{k,\lambda})^{s}\, |\hat f_{k,\lambda}|^2,
$$

where $\hat f_{k,\lambda}$ are the coefficients of $f$ in the $\Delta_b$
eigenbasis and $\sigma_{k,\lambda}=(2k+n)|\lambda|$. Equivalently
$\|f\|_{S_b^s}=\|(I+\Delta_b)^{s/2}f\|_{L^2}$.

### 6.2 Training loss

$$
\boxed{\;\mathcal L_\text{CR} = \|\mathrm{out}-y\|^2_{S_b^s} + \mu\,\|\bar\partial_b\,\mathrm{out}\|^2\;}
$$

- First term: CR-Sobolev regression/matching (replaces Euclidean $\|\cdot\|^2$),
  frequency-weighted, low frequencies (the information core) weighted higher.
- Second term: $\bar\partial_b$ energy regularization, pushing the output toward
  CR functions = compression to the holomorphic subspace.
- $\mu>0$ is the regularization strength (hyperparameter); $s$ the Sobolev order
  (default $s=1$).

### 6.3 Relation to the Euclidean loss

The Euclidean loss is the degenerate case $s=0,\mu=0$. The CR-Sobolev loss
weights the spectrum, i.e. imposes a "frequency-domain prior" on the
representation — a different acting domain from Transformer weight decay (which
acts on parameter space, not representation space).

## 7. Discretization and sequence embedding

### 7.1 Sequence → $\mathbb{H}^n$ lattice

A sequence of length $N$ maps to a lattice on the finite Heisenberg group.
Default $n=2$, on a $p\times p\times p$ lattice with $p\approx N^{1/3}$ (rounded
up/down, zero-padded).

Lattice coordinates $(x_a,y_b,t_c)$, $a,b,c\in\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$, give the
finite Heisenberg group $H_p$ (the $3\times 3$ upper-triangular matrix group,
order $p^3$). On it there is an $O(p^3\log p)=O(N\log N)$ FFT
(Diaconis–Rockmore 1990) — the engineering basis of the speedup.

> **Key constraint (v0.2 correction)**: $p$ **must be prime**. For composite $p$,
> the Schrödinger representations $\rho_\lambda$ are reducible when
> $\gcd(\lambda,p)>1$, and the whole family
> $\{\rho_\lambda:\lambda=1..p{-}1\}$ is **incomplete** — the low-dimensional
> irreps go missing, $\sum d_\rho^2 < |G|$, and Schur orthogonality, Plancherel,
> and the inversion formula all fail (measured: ~5% energy leakage, nonzero
> round-trip error).
>
> For prime $p$,
> $\{\rho_\lambda:\lambda=1..p{-}1\}$ (dimension $p$) $\cup$
> $\{\chi_{m,n}:m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_p\}$ ($p^2$ one-dimensional characters, the
> $\lambda=0$ sector) form a complete irrep set with
> $\sum d_\rho^2=(p{-}1)p^2+p^2=p^3=|G|$.
>
> Engineering consequence: choose neighbouring primes (3, 5, 7, 11, 13, ...). The
> geometry/kernels/spectrum theory is unchanged; only the lattice spacing
> differs. If a composite $p$ (e.g. $p=2^k$) is unavoidable, one must construct
> the missing projective irreps by hand — no clean uniform formula; the project
> does not support this.

### 7.2 Group law and representations (implementation-fixed)

The implemented group law (the one that makes the Schrödinger representation a
homomorphism; one-sided commutator, coefficient 1):

$$(a_1,b_1,c_1)\cdot(a_2,b_2,c_2)=(a_1{+}a_2,\ b_1{+}b_2,\ c_1{+}c_2{+}a_1 b_2)\ \bmod p$$

Inverse: $(a,b,c)^{-1}=(-a,\,-b,\,-c{+}ab)\bmod p$.

> **Note**: this is isomorphic but differently parametrized from the symmetric
> continuous form $t{+}t'{+}2\,\mathrm{Im}(z\bar z')$ (coefficient 2, two-sided).
> The implementation uniformly uses the one-sided form above; continuous kernels
> (Korányi/Szegő) are evaluated on the lattice under this law. Other places in
> the docs that write the continuous form are geometric intuition only; the
> implementation is authoritative.

Schrödinger representation ($\omega=e^{+2\pi i/p}$):

$$[\rho_\lambda(a,b,c)]_{u,v}=\omega^{\lambda c}\,\omega^{\lambda b u}\,\delta_{v,(u+a)\bmod p}$$

Group Fourier transform: scalar $f\mapsto$ matrix-valued $\hat f$,
- $\lambda=0$ sector: $\hat f(0;m,n)=\sum_{a,b,c}f(a,b,c)\,\omega^{ma+nb}$
  ($p^2$ scalars; $c$ invisible)
- $\lambda=1..p{-}1$ sector:
  $\hat f(\lambda)_{u,v}=\sum_{a,b,c}f(a,b,c)\,[\rho_\lambda(a,b,c)]_{u,v}$
  ($p\times p$ matrices)

Inversion (Plancherel; $|G|=p^3$, $d_\rho=p$ in the matrix sector, $d=1$ in the
character sector):

$$f(a,b,c)=\frac{1}{p^3}\Big[\sum_{m,n}\hat f(0;m,n)\,\omega^{-ma-nb}+\sum_{\lambda=1}^{p-1}p\sum_{u,v}\hat f(\lambda)_{u,v}\,\overline{[\rho_\lambda(a,b,c)]_{u,v}}\Big]$$

Group convolution theorem:
$(f*g)\widehat{}(\lambda)=\hat f(\lambda)\,\hat g(\lambda)$ (matrix product,
$\hat f$ on the left), and
$(f*g)\widehat{}(0;m,n)=\hat f(0;m,n)\cdot\hat g(0;m,n)$ (pointwise). The naive
$O(p^6)$ drops to $O(p^4)$ ($p-1$ matrix products of size $p\times p$ plus a 2D
FFT).

### 7.3 Token embedding

The feature dimension $d$ maps to a $d$-channel complex field
$f:G_p\to\mathbb{C}^d$ on the $\mathbb{H}^n$ lattice. The embedding layer is a
learnable linear map $\mathbb{R}^{d_\text{tok}}\to\mathbb{C}^{d\times p^3}$,
expanded by position.

## 8. Geometric analogy for softmax attention (narrative only, not mathematical equivalence)

Verified by survey:

- The softmax kernel $e^{q\cdot k}$ is structurally analogous to the Bargmann
  reproducing kernel $e^{z\bar w}=\sum(z\bar w)^m/m!$; both are exponential
  kernels.
- But **"softmax = a discrete truncation of the Bargmann coherent-state kernel"
  is not a proven equality**: the strict correspondence requires query/key to be
  a complex-conjugate pair, which the standard Transformer does not satisfy.
- **This project's narrative**: take the CR Szegő projection as a *geometric
  replacement* for attention (not a "geometric reinterpretation of softmax").
  Both are "kernel-regression information aggregation", but the CR kernel comes
  from the manifold's spectral structure while softmax's comes from an inner
  product. This is *replacement*, not *reinterpretation*.

> The paper and docs must **never** claim softmax equals the Bargmann kernel;
> only a structural analogy is permitted.
